Dissertation: From Perception to Clinical Reasoning: Disentangled, Evidence-Grounded Multimodal Learning for Robust Medical Image Analysis and Reporting (Advisor: Dr. Dong Hye Ye; proposal defended April 2026). Reading a medical image involves five judgments: locating a finding, grading it, weighing it against clinical context, comparing it with earlier studies, and committing to a claim. This dissertation builds methods for each stage on two ideas: every stage carries a distribution rather than a point estimate, and evidence from different sources is represented separately so that losing one source does not corrupt the others. The work spans attention as a learned spatial distribution (DCAN), ordinal and co-occurring label structure (CAP-WAE, sparse expert routing), disentangled vision-language learning under missing context (DREAM, DiA-gnostic VLVAE, SC-CoMoE), and uncertainty-aware longitudinal reporting with evidence-grounded temporal claims (Dist-RRG, STRIDE, DELTA). It is evaluated across endoscopy, whole-slide histology, retinal photography, brain MRI with genomics, and chest radiography.

Research & Development

My research makes multimodal medical AI hold up on messy, incomplete patient data. Reading a medical image means locating a finding, grading it, weighing it against clinical context, comparing it with earlier studies, and committing to a claim. I build methods for each step on two ideas: every step carries a distribution rather than a point estimate, and evidence from different sources is represented separately, so losing one source does not corrupt the others. The work spans attention and label distributions for perception, disentangled vision-language learning that stays well defined when clinical context is missing, and uncertainty-aware longitudinal reasoning whose temporal claims are grounded in image evidence. It is evaluated across endoscopy, histology, retinal photography, brain MRI with genomics, and chest radiography. Read my research statement.
